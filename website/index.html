AAC for children and young people

An AAC device built to be dropped.

Rugged Speech is AAC (augmentative and alternative communication) that children can take everywhere: big picture buttons on a tough tablet that can go to school, to the park and to bed. It's free, and it works without wifi.

Made for everyday life

Go on. Take it to the park, drop it in the grass.

Rugged Speech is a communication aid made for real childhoods: muddy knees, car journeys, wobbly tables and the odd drop. It's designed to be used all day, every day, wherever your child is, so it never needs to be kept on a shelf for best.

It runs on rugged Windows tablets, the chunky kind with rubber corners that are made to take a knock. That means your child's communication can come along to breakfast, to school, to Grandma's and to bed, with nobody worrying about breakages. It works on any Windows 10 or 11 device, and on Macs too, and every new feature arrives on both at the same time.

It's for children and young people who are non-speaking or minimally speaking, and for those whose speech comes and goes or takes a lot of effort. That includes autistic children, children with speech, language and communication needs (SLCN), and children and young people who stammer. Speech is always welcome alongside it: AAC supports talking, it doesn't replace it.

A day with Rugged Speech

4pm · In the garden

Playing, and telling you when it hurts.

Piano, Draw and Snap have no clocks and no scores, just play. If there's a tumble, My body lets your child point to where it hurts on a figure that looks like them, then say it.

Room to say the big things.

For parents and carers

From the box to your child's first words in 20 minutes.

You don't need to be good with computers. Every choice can be changed later, and there's a Back button on every step.

  1. Install it 2 minutes

    On Windows, double-click the file. If it says Windows protected your PC, press More info, then Run anyway. On a Mac, open the disk image and drag the app to Applications, then allow it once in System Settings, Privacy & Security. You won't need an administrator password on Windows.

  2. Answer a few questions 8 minutes

    Your child's name, a voice they like, how big the buttons are, and pictures or emoji. Then choose a four-number Parent PIN.

  3. Write down your recovery code

    It's four words, shown once. It's the only way back in if you forget your PIN, so keep it somewhere safe.

  4. Make it theirs 10 minutes

    Add Mum, Grandad, nursery and the park with real photos, and record your voice saying their names. For many children, this is the part they love most.

  5. Keep it safe

    Fill in Medical Info and the Lost mode details, then save a backup to a USB stick. The app reminds you once a month.

For speech and language therapists

Designed around good AAC practice. The clinical choices are left to you.

Rugged Speech doesn't try to decide what a child should say. It gives you a stable, flexible system, and leaves the vocabulary, staging and settings to you and the family.

Motor planning

Buttons never move

Grids never rearrange themselves. Hiding a word leaves an empty space, and making a page bigger only adds empty spaces. The grid size only changes when an adult changes it.

Core and fringe

A small core, personal folders

A stable first set, with folders of nine or fewer. Families add the fringe with photos and recorded voices.

Iwantlikemorenoyeshelpgostoplookagainthat
Aided language input

Made for modelling

Pressing a word speaks it, so partners can model as they talk. Sentences can be read one word at a time, with each word lit.

Colour coding

A Modified Fitzgerald Key

Colour follows word class on every page. You can adjust it, and the app warns you if two classes look too alike.

Staged vocabulary

Word stages and focus words

Bring words in gradually without moving anything, and outline the words being practised. No word comes with a stage set.

Expectant delay

Time to answer

Give me time is always one press away. Prediction only suggests, and nothing speaks unless the child presses.

Access

More than one way to press

One- and two-switch scanning, hold-to-select, ignoring accidental double presses, keyboard access, high contrast and text size.

Speech output

A voice that fits

Choose the voice, speed and pitch. "Say it like this" teaches it to pronounce names, and recorded clips can replace the synthetic voice.

Evidence

Data you can use, if you want it

Optional word counts and an activity record, both off by default, export to a spreadsheet. Pages share as Open Board Format files.

The starter words need a therapist's eye.

The starter vocabulary is a sensible beginning, but no speech and language therapist has reviewed it yet. It hasn't been tested with children either. The clinical review lists the decisions we think belong to a clinician. If you'd be willing to look at it, please email us at aac@communitystack.io.

Getting started: parents and carers

Rugged Speech has big buttons with pictures and words. When your child presses them, the tablet says the words out loud. It works just the same with the wifi off.

What you need

  • A Windows 10 or 11 tablet or computer, or a Mac. A touchscreen is best, but a mouse works.
  • The installer: Rugged Speech Test Setup 0.0.1.exe on Windows, or the disk image for your Mac (Apple silicon or Intel).
  • About 10 minutes, and a quiet moment to choose a voice with your child.

The first set-up

  1. Whose device is this? A name makes it "Lucy's device", shown along the top, so anyone who finds it knows whose it is.
  2. Choose a voice. Press Try it and pick the one your child likes best.
  3. Choose how big the buttons are. Fewer, bigger buttons are easier. Once it's set, try not to change it.
  4. Pictures and words. Drawn pictures or emoji; pictures and words, pictures only, or words only.
  5. What pressing does. For a child just starting, Say the word straight away is usually best.
  6. Colours, then a Parent PIN. Write down the four-word recovery code.

10 minutes in Parent Mode

  1. People and Places: add the people and places your child talks about, with a photo and your voice.
  2. My body: make the figure look like your child, including hair, a head covering such as a hijab, turban or kippah, a wheelchair or other equipment.
  3. About me and Medical: anyone can read these with one press, so only write what you're happy to share.
  4. Quick Access: choose the six top buttons, such as Break, Toilet, Games or Traffic light.
  5. Look and Seasons: pick a favourite colour, and choose which celebrations your child sees in Games.
  6. School: only if a school will use it. Turn on School Mode and give staff a School PIN.

A few good habits

  • Wait. After you ask something, give plenty of time before asking again.
  • Keep buttons where they are. Knowing where a button is, is how a child gets quick.
  • Start small. A few words your child really wants (more, drink, help, stop) beat a page full.
  • Ask your speech and language therapist to look at the words and settings with you.

Getting started: teachers and staff

For teachers, teaching assistants, key workers and SENCos. Each child has their own device, with their own words, pictures, voice and settings. There are no pupil accounts or class lists.

Turning School Mode on (once)

  1. Whoever has the Parent PIN opens Parent Mode, then School, then Turn School Mode on…
  2. They choose a four-number School PIN, to share only with the staff who need it.
  3. A School Mode button appears next to Parent Mode. Nothing on the child's screen moves.

School Mode closes itself after 10 minutes without use. If the School PIN is forgotten, the parent can choose a new one, so nobody gets locked out.

Your first 10 minutes

  1. Look at Today.
  2. Fill in Pupil and school.
  3. Read Safeguarding and add your designated safeguarding lead (DSL).
  4. Choose any Classroom set-up options you want.
  5. Build the Timetable.
  6. Add Lesson pages from the ready-made ones.
  7. Agree the Vocabulary with the child's therapist.
  8. Set a first Target.
  9. Agree who writes Notes.
  10. Print a Handover sheet for supply staff.

Supporting communication

  • Model it. Press the buttons yourself as you talk ("Let's get your coat on"). You don't need to ask the child to press anything.
  • Wait. Count to ten in your head. Give me time lets the child tell you they're thinking.
  • Respect the traffic light. Red: please leave me. Amber: talk gently and wait. Green: happy to talk.
  • Treat a break request as communication, and give the break.

Getting the tablet ready

  1. Install the English (UK) voices in Settings → Time and language → Speech, while you have wifi. After that, no internet is needed.
  2. Turn the volume up, and try the voices during Rugged Speech's first set-up.
  3. Set a Windows sign-in (or a Mac login), and turn on device encryption if it's available (BitLocker, or FileVault on a Mac). The Parent PIN protects the settings, not the device itself.
  4. Label the tablet with your child's name, and fill in Lost mode in Parent Mode.

Setting up Rugged Speech

Keep this by the tablet, or inside the front cover of the child's communication folder.

Everyday use

  • The six buttons along the top (to begin with: Home, Help, Yes, No, Favourites, Keyboard) are always there. Help can never be removed.
  • Every screen except Home has a Home button at the top left.
  • Give me time says "I know what I want to say. Please give me a moment."
  • Parent Mode (top right) opens the settings: one press, then your PIN.
  • Nothing is ever said unless somebody presses something.

The three best things to do in the first week

  1. Press the buttons yourself as you talk, so your child sees how it works. Then wait.
  2. Add the people and places that matter, with real photos and your own voice.
  3. Fill in About me and Medical, and save a backup.

Questions people often ask

Does it need wifi?

No. It never uses the internet. New versions come as a new installer that you choose to run.

Where is my child's information kept?

On the tablet only. The only way anything leaves it is a file an adult chooses to save, such as a backup.

Does it cost anything?

No. Rugged Speech is free, with no subscription and no in-app purchases.

Can school staff see what my child said?

Only if the activity record or Recent is turned on. Both are off to begin with, and both have a Clear button and an off switch.

Can a school set it up for a whole class?

No. It's one device for one child. A good set-up can be saved as a backup, and single pages can be shared as files.

Does the Parent PIN lock the tablet?

No. It keeps the settings safe. Your child can still switch to other programs. Use your Windows or Mac sign-in to protect the device itself.
